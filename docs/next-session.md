## White-balance fix: seeded, root cause found, materially better but not fully solved (31 August - 2 September 2026 - see also priority item 2)

The session-start colour cast (finding above) was reported as a repeated problem, not a one-off.

**Attempt 1** (28-29 August 2026): explicit AWB config plus an active 15 s frame-pump warm-up instead of a bare `delay()`. Trialled in real daylight 29 August. **Result: the mechanism worked as designed (85 real frames pumped, correct AWB state confirmed) but didn't solve the problem** - the cast (purple/magenta this time, not green) was still present at session start and still took ~27-30 minutes to clear.

**Attempt 2** (31 August 2026, owner's own diagnosis): the 85-frame warm-up was rate-limited (only ~5.7 fps at full QXGA resolution), not time-limited. Fix: warm up in a small, fast, still-colour sensor mode (`CameraService::configureWarmupSensor()`, QQVGA JPEG) before switching to the real capture mode - reused the project's existing dual-resolution-mode pattern. **Measured on-device: 13.8 fps (138 frames in 10 s), ~2.4x the previous rate.** A follow-up trial showed this genuinely speeds up the *first* stage of the transition (reaches the purple/magenta stage in under a minute, versus several minutes before) but a second, longer stage (purple/magenta → neutral) is unaffected - it still runs at the session's normal ~1 fps capture cadence once warm-up ends, so most of the ~1,600-1,800 frames needed are still bought at the slow rate. **The owner's under-15-second target is not reachable this way** - at the measured 13.8 fps ceiling, reaching full convergence during warm-up alone would need roughly 2 minutes, not 15 seconds. Full detail and the actual images compared: [hardware-validation.md](hardware-validation.md) "Fix attempted: fast low-resolution warm-up mode".

**Attempt 3** (31 August 2026, same day, owner's own proposal): seed AWB from a known-good gain, then let it walk from that seed to whatever's optimal for actual current light. Fetched the real OV3660 driver source (`sensors/ov3660.c`, `espressif/esp32-camera`) rather than guessing register addresses - confirmed manual R/G/B gain at `0x3400`/`0x3402`/`0x3404`, reachable via the sensor's own `get_reg`/`set_reg`. Added `CameraService::manualGainStatus()`/`seedWhiteBalance()`.

**Found the actual root cause of why attempt 2 didn't stick**, via direct register readback before/after a mode switch: `esp_camera_init()` resets manual gain to `1024/1024/1024` on every call. Attempt 2's fast-mode warm-up converged nicely, then threw it all away switching back to the real capture mode. Removed that two-mode design entirely.

**A calibration pump held continuously in the real capture mode (no switch) plateaued within ~10 seconds and stayed flat for a full 6-minute test** - undercutting the earlier "~1,700 frames" theory; the register itself isn't what took ~27-30 minutes to settle in the original finding, something else in the pipeline likely is. The plateaued value (`run_000078`, this room, daylight) produced a **visually neutral image** - confirmed by eye, not just by the register reading.

**Shipped:** `seedWhiteBalance()` writes that measured value directly onto the real capture sensor at boot (no mode switch, so no reset risk), then a 12 s pump lets AWB walk from there to actual current conditions - meeting the owner's original under-15-second ask. `kCameraWarmupMs = 12000`.

**The 31 August on-device check landed after dark** (a genuinely black/noisy image, not a colour-cast problem), so it didn't confirm the shipped path.

**Daylight re-check, 2 September 2026 (`run_000082`):** a real, visible improvement over every pre-seed trial - much weaker than the green/purple/magenta casts seen before - but not a clean pass. A mild residual purple/mauve tint remains, mostly in shadow, stable across two frames pulled several seconds apart (not still drifting). Gain landed at `r=1524 g=1024 b=1386` after the 12 s pump, a large swing from the `r=1055 g=1024 b=2100` seed - AWB is clearly still moving a meaningful distance in 12 s, not just fine-tuning near the seed. **Closed as "materially better, not solved"** rather than pushed further today - a longer post-seed pump (30-60 s) is the obvious next experiment if anyone wants to chase full neutrality; see priority item 2 at the top of this file.

### Issue 6: why the fix reconciles the display and does not rewrite the card

The owner asked whether the dashboard could check the record against the card on load and, if they disagree, rewrite the record to match the images actually present - and explicitly asked for feedback on that specific idea.

**Recommendation taken: reconcile what is displayed, never rewrite the card.** Three reasons, in order of weight:

- **Rewriting would destroy evidence.** `raw/captures.csv` is the authoritative log of every capture *attempt*, deliberately including ones that produced no image ([data-schema.md](data-schema.md), [reconciliation-policy.md](reconciliation-policy.md)). A record whose JPEG is gone is not a wrong record - it is a true record of a capture whose file was later removed. Deleting those rows would make a card that has lost data look like a card that never had it, which is exactly the failure mode the retention policy exists to prevent. `tests/check_project.py` already pins "dashboard must not promise browser-side deletion" for this reason.
- **A browser cannot tell "removed" from "not visible from here".** The page is opened from the card, but the browser only gets a file listing after the folder picker is used - and the folder it is handed may be the wrong one. Rewriting on that basis would let one mis-click erase a card's index.
- **It is not needed to fix what was actually wrong.** The complaint was that the page *displays* more pictures than exist. Filtering the display fixes that completely and reversibly.

**What was built:** a "Picture check" band under the metric tiles. Pressing "Check the card" uses the same single folder choice as everything else; the page then compares each record's image path against the files present and shows only what is really there - picture counts, the welcome line, the gallery, the total-recorded-time tile, and the frames table, which now marks those rows "No longer on the card". It states plainly how many are missing and that nothing on the card was changed. Until the check is run, behaviour is exactly as before. If the chosen folder contains none of this card's pictures, the result is rejected as a wrong-folder mistake, any earlier good check is left untouched, and the page says so.

**Deliberately not done, and where it belongs if it is ever wanted:** an actual rebuild of the on-card record. [reconciliation-policy.md](reconciliation-policy.md) already specifies the shape that would have to take - "a future quarantine/rebuild command must require an explicit destination, refuse to operate on a camera-mounted card, create a log, and support a dry run". That is a `tools/` script run by an adult with a backup, not a button in a children's dashboard.

**One thing left consistent on purpose:** the motion-watch panel still counts what the camera decided during the run, not what survives on the card. It is a chart of the camera's saving decisions at the time, not a claim about files present. Worth revisiting only if it ever confuses someone.

**Also fixed while in here** (not on the owner's list, found during the work): `dashboard/demo.html` had every emoji, ellipsis and arrow flattened to literal `?` characters by some earlier encoding accident - visible as "??" all over the Barcelona rehearsal demo. Restored to the same HTML entities `dashboard.html` uses. Separately, the front page previously stated two different picture totals (the hero line counted capture *attempts*, the tiles counted images); both now count images.

## Toolchain note: invoking PlatformIO on this Windows machine

Windows Device Guard blocks the `pio.exe`/`platformio.exe` launcher stubs directly (`ApplicationFailedException: Your organization used Device Guard to block this app`), whichever Python installed them. Always invoke PlatformIO as a Python module through the signed `py` launcher instead:

```powershell
py -m platformio run
py -m platformio run -e xiao_esp32s3 -t upload --upload-port COM4
```

This already matches the command form used in [performance-experiment.md](performance-experiment.md); the gotcha is that plain `pio ...` or `platformio ...` will fail on this machine even though `py -m platformio --version` and `py -m platformio run` work normally. `py --version` (Python 3.13) on *this* machine already has PlatformIO Core installed, so no extra setup is required here.

On a fresh machine, `py -m pip install platformio` is the one setup step — confirmed 9 September 2026 on a clean Windows 10 box, where a full build + upload + boot of `run` firmware succeeded end to end after that single command. Note that installing *only* the PlatformIO VS Code extension leaves `py -m platformio` unable to find PlatformIO (`No module named platformio`), because the extension keeps its own isolated `penv`. The end-user [setup guide](setup-guide.md) and the [software README](../README.md) were corrected to install Core with `py -m pip install platformio` explicitly rather than implying the VS Code extension is sufficient.

**New-device setup is now one script (10 September 2026): `py tools\setup_device.py`.** It auto-detects the board's serial port, wraps the PlatformIO upload, then installs the card (dashboard, `vendor/mediabunny`, folders, pilot `config.json`) via `prepare_card()` — the function factored out of `prepare_sd.py` so there is one source of truth. Flags `--flash-only`, `--skip-flash --card <root>`, `--port`, `--card`, `--yes` cover the scripted and partial cases. Verified end to end on a fresh board + blank card (see [hardware-validation.md](hardware-validation.md), 10 September 2026 entry). Still not automated: FAT32 formatting (the script only warns), and the ~650 KB `mediabunny` file cannot go over the dev bridge (256 KB write cap), so a card that must be prepared remotely loses only the in-browser movie export until it next sees a reader.

If `py -m platformio` is ever unavailable in a fresh environment, the PlatformIO VS Code extension's bundled portable Python can bootstrap a working copy without admin rights: extract `<extension-dir>\assets\predownloaded\python-portable-*.tar.gz` to `%USERPROFILE%\.platformio\python3`, run that `python.exe -m ensurepip`, then `python.exe -m venv %USERPROFILE%\.platformio\penv`. The resulting `penv\Scripts\python.exe -m platformio` works the same way (module invocation, not the `.exe` stub, which is blocked the same way).

## SD write performance (in progress, 27 August 2026)

The SD SPI clock now steps 25 → 20 → 10 → 4 MHz and keeps the first that mounts; the card in use accepts 25 MHz. Image write time roughly halved (758–777 ms → 382–405 ms). Full measurements and reasoning in [performance-experiment.md](performance-experiment.md).

**Resume here:**

1. ~~`kPerformanceSampleInterval` (100, `src/main.cpp`) collides with `DashboardWriter::kChunkSize` (100)~~ **Fixed 28 August 2026**: changed to 97 (prime), verified live over the dev bridge - a fresh sample landed on capture 97 with `dashboard_ms=26` (not the ~447 ms promotion cost) and `total_ms=732`. See [performance-experiment.md](performance-experiment.md). Only one data point so far; a longer run would give a real distribution.
2. Reduce the write path. Metadata now costs more per frame than the JPEG: chunk promotion does four file operations at once; each frame opens, flushes and closes three separate files; and the atomic write does `remove` → `open` → `write` → `flush` → `close` → `rename`.
3. exFAT was suggested by a collaborator for write throughput. The reasoning is sound but it is unavailable in our toolchain (`FF_FS_EXFAT 0` in the shipped ESP-IDF), and adopting it means rebuilding ESP-IDF or replacing `SD.h` with SdFat. Revisit only if metadata still dominates after step 2.

Note `total_ms` sat at 983–1030 ms against the 1,000 ms budget at 1 FPS on *promotion* frames specifically (that measurement is still accurate for that case); the first typical-frame figure is 732 ms, comfortably under budget - cadence headroom looks better than previously measured, but confirm with more than one sample before relying on it.

### 2. Plug board directly into phone as SD storage

The intended next-generation workflow is low-touch: plug the powered-off camera board into a phone, let the board expose its physical SD card as USB storage, open the dashboard, approve access/write once, then choose safe configuration or analysis actions. The external-card-reader route is not a product direction.

1. **ESP32-S3 board-as-storage feasibility spike:** implement or prototype native USB Mass Storage Class (MSC) presenting the physical SD card to the phone. The board must enter an explicit USB-storage mode: stop capture, flush and unmount the card, then expose it. On disconnect it must remount, reconcile, and record an intentional hand-off/recovery event. Never allow capture and USB-host access to the filesystem at the same time.
2. **Integrated child journey:** minimise steps to connect the board, open the dashboard, approve storage access/write, choose a safe preset, and safely disconnect. Automatic browser launching on USB attachment may be investigated, but cannot be assumed until demonstrated on target phones.
3. **Acceptance testing:** test named Android/Chrome and iPhone/Safari devices; normal and interrupted disconnects; at least 50 USB hand-offs; phone power/brown-out behaviour; SD-card integrity; configuration survival after board reboot; and dashboard write/read round trips.

If direct browser writing or the USB-storage hand-off cannot pass on the required phone/browser combination, stop rather than reverting to a high-user-involvement workflow. Consider a native companion application or board-hosted configuration interface only through a later product decision.
